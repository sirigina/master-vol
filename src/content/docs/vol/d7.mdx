Gamma and theta are not two separate risks. They are two sides of one bet: **will SPY move more or less than the option price assumes?** This lesson makes that bet precise, puts a dollar figure on the daily breakeven, and checks it against what SPY actually did.

## One equation ties gamma to theta

<Plain>

A hedged long option is a deal: you pay **rent** every day (the melting ice, theta) and in return you collect a payoff whenever SPY makes a move (the accelerator, gamma). The price of the option sets the rent at exactly the level where, if SPY moves as much as the implied volatility forecasts, you break even.

So a hedged option is a pure bet on the **weather that happens versus the forecast**. Bigger swings than forecast: the long side wins. Smaller swings: the short side wins. Direction doesn't matter.

</Plain>

<Pro>

For a delta-hedged option in the Black-Scholes world, the pricing equation itself says

$$
\Theta + \tfrac{1}{2}\,\Gamma\,S^2\sigma^2 + rS\Delta - rV = 0
$$

For a delta-hedged position the financing terms ($$rS\Delta - rV$$) are small near the money over a day, which leaves the core relation

$$
\Theta \approx -\tfrac{1}{2}\,\Gamma\,S^2\sigma^2
$$

with $$\Theta$$ per year. Over one day, the hedged P/L is therefore

$$
\Delta\Pi \approx \tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma\left[(\Delta S)^2 - S^2\sigma^2\,\Delta t\right]
$$

Positive when the realized squared move beats the implied one. Check it on the snapshot: the 770 straddle has gamma 0.0252 at 14.06% volatility, so $$\tfrac{1}{2}\times 0.0252\times 769.66^2\times 0.1406^2/365 = 0.404$$ per calendar day. The model's straddle theta is **0.409**. The small difference is the financing term.

</Pro>

## The daily breakeven move

Set $$(\Delta S)^2 = S^2\sigma^2\Delta t$$ and solve: the hedged position breaks even on a day when SPY moves

$$
\Delta S^{*} = S\,\sigma\sqrt{\Delta t} = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{252}}\,S
$$

At 14.06% and SPY 769.66, that is **$6.82**, the same "rule of 16" daily move from [lesson C2](/vol/c2/). Any day SPY moves more than about $6.80 pays the long straddle's rent; any quieter day doesn't.

<BreakevenMove client:visible />

At the snapshot's volatility, ten hedged 770 straddles cost about **$599** of rent per trading day, and the full reprice puts the breakeven at +$6.96 and −$6.70 (slightly asymmetric because gamma is a little higher below the strike). Slide implied volatility down to 10% and the rent falls to about $431 a day with a ±$4.90 breakeven; at 20% the rent is about $846 and the breakeven near ±$9.70. Higher implied volatility means a higher bar for the long side to clear.

Note the clock: this chart uses one **trading** day (1/252 of a year), so each weekday's rent includes a share of weekend decay. On a calendar-day basis the theta is $409 per day for ten straddles and the breakeven is about $5.70, which is why [lesson D2](/vol/d2/) found ±$5.70 for a single calendar day.

<Plain>

Here is the deal in numbers. Owning ten hedged 770 straddles costs about $600 a trading day in melt. SPY has to move about **$6.80** on a given day, in either direction, to pay that day's rent. Move $14 (twice the breakeven) and you collect about four times the rent; sit still and you lose the rent.

</Plain>

<Pro>

The trade-off scales cleanly:

- Rent per day is $$\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma S^2\sigma_{\text{implied}}^2/252$$; payoff per day is $$\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma(\Delta S)^2$$.
- Summed over many days, the hedged P/L is about $$\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_t \Gamma_t S_t^2(r_t^2 - \sigma_{\text{implied}}^2\Delta t)$$: a **gamma-weighted** difference between realized and implied variance. Moves that happen when gamma is large (near the strike, near expiry) count most. [Lesson E1](/vol/e1/) builds on this.

</Pro>

## Rent vs payoff: SPY's last 60 days

Replay SPY's actual daily moves against the rent. The position here is one hedged straddle with the 770 straddle's snapshot gamma (0.0252) held constant, so each day's payoff is exactly $$\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma(\Delta S)^2$$ and each day's rent is fixed by the implied volatility you choose.

<RentVsPayoff client:visible />
<SnapshotNote detail="SPY daily closes Aug 3 to Oct 27, 2026; gamma from the 770 straddle, Nov 27 expiry" />

Over the 60 trading days to October 27, only **7 days** beat the $6.82 breakeven, and SPY's realized volatility (measured as the root-mean-square daily move) was **9.9%**. At 14.06% implied, the long straddle collected about **$1,732** of gamma payoff against **$3,508** of rent per straddle: the seller won. Slide implied volatility down to 9.9% and the two lines meet. That is the general rule: **a hedged long option makes money if realized volatility beats the implied volatility paid**.

Shorten the window to 10 days. Realized volatility over the last two weeks rose to 11.8%, with three days beyond $6.82 (October 14, 16 and 22): closer, but still short of 14%.

<Plain>

The market forecast stormy weather (14%), and the weather was calmer (about 10%). Anyone who sold the forecast collected the difference; anyone who bought it paid for storms that didn't come. This is the most common pattern in index options, and also the one that hurts sellers most when it breaks: the March 2026 selloff would have reversed the picture.

</Plain>

<Pro>

The constant-gamma replay is a clean teaching device, not a backtest. In a real hedged straddle, gamma rises as expiry nears and falls if SPY drifts away from 770, so the gamma-weighted realized variance depends on the path. Discrete daily rehedging adds noise around the expected P/L ([lesson E2](/vol/e2/)). And sixty days is a small sample: the standard error of a 60-day realized volatility estimate is roughly $$\sigma/\sqrt{2n} \approx 9\%$$ of its level, about ±0.9 points here.

</Pro>

## Elasticity: options as leverage

Gamma vs theta is the hedger's view. The directional trader's view of the same trade-off is **leverage**: how many percent the option moves for a 1% move in SPY.

<Pro>

Elasticity (also called lambda or leverage) is

$$
\lambda = \frac{\partial V / V}{\partial S / S} = \Delta \cdot \frac{S}{V}
$$

The 770 call: $$0.541 \times 769.66 / 13.90 = 30.0$$. A 1% SPY rise ($7.70) moves the call about 30%, from $13.90 to roughly $18. The 800 call: $$0.158 \times 769.66 / 2.14 = 56.8$$. The 700 call, deep in the money: 9.5. Elasticity measures leverage for small moves only; gamma and theta then change it.

</Pro>

<Elasticity client:visible />
<SnapshotNote detail="Nov 27, 2026 expiry, vendor deltas, mid prices" />

<Plain>

Cheaper, further out-of-the-money options give more **bang per dollar** for a move in the right direction. The 800 call moves about 57% for each 1% in SPY. But that leverage is paid for with a high probability of losing everything, and a faster melt relative to its price. The put side tops out around 34× (the 740 put) because OTM puts carry much higher implied volatility (the skew), which makes them more expensive per unit of delta.

</Plain>

Leverage and the gamma-theta trade-off are the same fact seen two ways. High elasticity means a small premium buys a lot of exposure, and a small premium also means time decay eats a large share of the position each day.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating theta as free money.** It is payment for the gamma risk you are short. The relation $$\Theta \approx -\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma S^2\sigma^2$$ says the two are priced against each other.
- **Comparing realized and implied without matching the clock.** Use 252 trading days for daily moves; theta on a calendar-day basis looks smaller on weekdays and larger over weekends.
- **Reading the last month as the next month.** Implied above realized for 60 days does not guarantee the next 60; it is the volatility risk premium, and it can reverse violently.
- **Confusing leverage with edge.** A 57× elasticity amplifies outcomes; it does not improve the odds.

<Takeaway>

- For a delta-hedged option, $$\Theta \approx -\tfrac{1}{2}\Gamma S^2\sigma^2$$: theta is the price of gamma at the implied volatility.
- Daily breakeven move = $$S\sigma/\sqrt{252}$$, about $6.82 for SPY at 14.06%.
- Long gamma wins when realized volatility beats implied; short gamma wins when it doesn't.
- Over the 60 days to Oct 27, SPY realized about 9.9% vs 14.1% implied; a hedged long straddle collected about half its rent.
- Elasticity λ = Δ·S/V: 30× for the 770 call, 57× for the 800 call. Leverage and decay rise together.

</Takeaway>
